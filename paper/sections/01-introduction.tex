\section{Introduction}

Cost-based query optimization depends on cardinality estimates to compare
candidate plans. Ordinary per-column statistics often approximate predicates
as independent even when attributes are correlated. Extended statistics provide
multivariate information that can improve this situation, including most-common
values and functional dependencies in PostgreSQL~\cite{ilyas2004cords,postgresql16docs}.
Choosing which extended statistics to maintain is therefore itself a physical
design problem. The choice changes the information available to optimization,
and deployed objects also incur recurring collection and refresh work. A useful
design must balance estimator utility against a maintenance capacity rather
than treating statistics collection as a free preprocessing step.

The difficulty is not only the size of the candidate universe. An extended
statistics object's effect is contextual. Changing the selected set can change
which objects are applicable, which overlapping object wins, which clauses or
semantic dimensions are consumed, whether a downstream MCV or functional-
dependency stage remains reachable, and how payload-dependent numerical
updates are applied. A statistic that is useful in isolation can become
irrelevant after another object consumes its dimensions; a statistic that is
negative as a singleton can become useful after a different object changes the
residual state. The response need not be additive or monotone. Consequently,
singleton scores and static per-object benefit models are insufficient for
reliably evaluating a hypothetical design.

This paper builds on, rather than reclaims, established ideas in workload-driven
statistics management, physical-design interactions, what-if analysis,
configuration-parametric optimization, and dependency-aware incremental
computation~\cite{chaudhuri2001statistics,schnaitter2009interactions,chaudhuri1998whatif,papadomanolakis2007inum,bruno2008cpqo,liu2016incremental}.
Those lines of work establish why design objects can interact, how a system can
evaluate hypothetical configurations, and how unnecessary recomputation can be
avoided. The narrower challenge addressed here is the represented object. The
object is the statistics-sensitive estimator transition system itself. It
cannot be replaced by a fixed response table or by a winner trace observed for
one design, because changing the selected set can alter future control and
downstream semantic state. The contribution is thus not a new generic
configuration-parametric or incremental-computation theory; it is an explicit,
executable representation of the native-semantic transitions needed by this
statistics physical-design task.

We call this representation \emph{CE-Replay}. CE-Replay is workload-specialized,
design-parametric, and executable. It freezes workload-fixed context such as
the supplied clauses, relation context, and ordinary selectivities, while
leaving design-dependent applicability, winner selection, clause and
semantic-dimension consumption, residual state, MCV-to-FD composition,
payload-dependent numerical updates, and relevant realization/precedence state
executable for every hypothetical selected set. The representation is native-
semantic within a stated PostgreSQL 16.14 fragment; it is not a complete
PostgreSQL estimator or a source-to-source compiler.

The same executable transition system exposes two interfaces. Its \textbf{objective
oracle} computes the cardinality estimate and workload loss induced by a
hypothetical physical statistics state. Its \textbf{semantic dependency oracle}
computes counterfactual and structural dependencies: which queries and semantic
dimensions may change when a design move is proposed. These dependencies are
not merely a log of what happened in one realized trace. They arise from the
same transitions that produce the estimate, so the system can preserve the
control and numerical consequences of a move while avoiding semantically
irrelevant recomputation. In particular, the same executable transition system
both determines the hypothetical estimate and exposes what semantic state may
change under a design move.

CE-Replay is the core mechanism of \emph{pg-extstats-advisor}. The system keeps
the production trust zone deliberately small: stock PostgreSQL remains
unpatched, a read-only production-side capture exports a sealed frozen
realization, and an offline advisor consumes that bundle without production
credentials. The advisor evaluates hypothetical definitions and payloads,
returns a recommendation in standard PostgreSQL statistics DDL, and leaves
preflight, deployment, \texttt{ANALYZE}, verification, and optional rollback
verification to a DBA-controlled lifecycle. This separation makes the
physical-design evaluator useful even when production is unavailable during
advice; Docker clean-room execution is used as a reproducibility check for the
workflow, not as the scientific contribution.

The realization boundary is important for interpreting correctness. Replay
correctness is defined for a matched, persisted realization: under the same
payload and effective precedence, CE-Replay should reproduce the supported
native estimate. A later native \texttt{ANALYZE} can produce a different fresh
realization, with different payloads, estimates, or preferred designs. Frozen
and fresh payloads therefore need not be identical. Differences across
independent realizations are treated as a robustness question, not as a failure
of same-realization replay fidelity, and the paper does not claim native-sample
equivalence or design invariance.

Search is a replaceable consumer of the two CE-Replay interfaces. Our current
advisor uses deterministic contextual maintenance-constrained search: greedy
construction followed by ADD/DROP/SWAP local improvement under a fixed
precedence and supplied workload. The search makes the contextual semantics
operational, but it is not the primary novelty and no global optimum is
claimed. The representation and its oracles remain useful to other consumers
that require a faithful hypothetical estimate or a safe semantic dependency
scope.

We evaluate the system along four dimensions. First, semantic-fidelity tests
separate frozen-realization correctness from fresh-realization fidelity after
physical deployment. Second, Census evaluates contextual candidate utility,
including singleton signs, contextual rescues, non-monotone behavior, and a
maintenance-feasible mixed design. Third, full and incremental replay are
compared for exact move values and decisions, semantic work reduction, and
operation-level timing at explicitly bounded measurement points. Fourth, the
production-separated lifecycle is exercised through capture, unavailable-source
offline advice, preflight, stock PostgreSQL deployment, verification,
rollback, and clean-room reproduction, while independent DMV realizations
provide bounded robustness evidence. These dimensions establish the supported
claim boundary; they do not imply query-latency improvement, universal
robustness, or general PostgreSQL coverage.

This paper makes four contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Executable statistics-sensitive CE representation.} We preserve
the supported PostgreSQL estimator's design-dependent applicability, winner
selection, consumption, MCV-to-FD composition, payload-dependent numerical
updates, and relevant precedence state as executable transitions over a
hypothetical statistics design.
\item \textbf{Dual semantic interfaces.} We derive an objective oracle and a
counterfactual semantic dependency oracle from that same transition system,
without claiming a new generic incremental-computation framework.
\item \textbf{Production-separated advisor architecture.} We connect
read-only capture from stock PostgreSQL to sealed offline advice and standard
DBA-controlled recommendation and verification artifacts.
\item \textbf{Validation and evidence.} We provide bounded evidence for
semantic fidelity, contextual physical design, exact incremental evaluation,
physical deployment closure, clean-room workflow reproduction, and independent-
realization robustness.
\end{enumerate}

The validated scope is PostgreSQL 16.14, one base relation, a fixed supplied
statistics target, arity-two MCV and functional-dependency statistics, and the
supported predicate fragment. Broader mechanisms, joins, automatic target
optimization, global search optimality, and runtime objectives remain outside
this paper's claims.
