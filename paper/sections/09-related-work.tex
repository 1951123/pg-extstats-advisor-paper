\section{Related Work}

This paper builds on several established lines rather than claiming the
selection problem, hypothetical objects, or offline tuning workflow as new.
The relevant distinction is the boundary at which PostgreSQL extended-
statistics state becomes hypothetical while PostgreSQL remains the semantic
authority.

\subsection{Automated statistics management}

Automated statistics management already treats statistics as workload-
dependent physical-design objects.  MNSA uses workload, optimizer, and data
information to select statistics while considering creation and maintenance
overhead~\cite{chaudhuri2001statistics}.  StatAdvisor recommends interacting
statistical views for a workload, and DB2's statistical views and column-group
facilities provide multivariate statistics-bearing objects matched by the
optimizer~\cite{elhelw2009statadvisor,db2statviews}.  These systems establish
workload-driven selection, maintenance concerns, multivariate objects, and
configuration-dependent utility.  The statistics-selection problem itself is
therefore not a contribution of this paper.  Our RQ3 experiments characterize
contextual utility in the supported PostgreSQL MCV/FD configuration space; they
do not discover statistics interaction in general or introduce a new advisor
objective.

\subsection{What-if physical design and hypothetical objects}

What-if physical design likewise predates this work.  AutoAdmin's What-If
Index Analysis Utility evaluates workload effects of hypothetical physical
configurations without materializing each index~\cite{chaudhuri1998whatif},
and the Database Engine Tuning Advisor evaluates user-specified hypothetical
indexes, indexed views, or partitioning against a workload~\cite{microsoftdta}.
HypoPG provides the closest PostgreSQL design pattern: hypothetical indexes
are planner-visible and backend-local, with session-scoped activation and
reset~\cite{hypopg}.  The hypothetical-object pattern is therefore established
prior art; this paper does not claim to be the first what-if interface or the
first backend-local hypothetical object.

The narrower distinction is the state consumed by the native estimator.
Within the validated PostgreSQL 16.14 fragment, the advisor derives native
MCV/FD payload states, retains their acquisition identity, represents a
legitimate \texttt{ABSENT\_NATIVE} result, and exposes an ordered active subset
of candidate objects to PostgreSQL's extended-statistics lookup path.  Native
PostgreSQL then performs the supported applicability, consumption,
deserialization, composition, and cardinality estimation.  HypoPG provides
the relevant backend-local hypothetical-object precedent, but for hypothetical
indexes rather than the native MCV/FD payload state studied here.  This is a
descriptive systems distinction, not a priority claim and not an assertion
that HypoPG cannot be extended.

\subsection{Pending, imported, restored, and statistical-view state}

Precomputed statistics state also has established testing and transport
mechanisms.  Oracle supports pending statistics selected by session policy and
supports export and import of collected optimizer statistics
\cite{oraclependingstats,oracleimportstats}.  Thus testing non-default or
pre-gathered statistics, session-scoped statistics testing, and statistics
transport are not novel claims here.  DB2 statistical views and column groups
further establish multiple statistics-bearing objects, optimizer matching, and
workload recommendation~\cite{db2statviews}; this paper does not imply that
those systems are weaker in general.

\begin{sloppypar}
PostgreSQL itself now documents
\texttt{pg\_restore\_}\allowbreak\texttt{extended\_stats()}, and
the corresponding PostgreSQL commit material describes creating or updating
restored extended-statistics values without requiring an immediate
\texttt{ANALYZE}~\cite{postgresql19restore,postgresql19restorecommit}.  This
directly overlaps with native extended-statistics payload restoration.  We do
not claim that PostgreSQL cannot restore extended statistics without
\texttt{ANALYZE}, that payload injection is novel, or that the PG16.14 patch is
necessary for future PostgreSQL versions.  The distinction is workflow and
state lifetime, not a priority claim about native payload restoration:
\texttt{pg-extstats-advisor} keeps an advisor-side candidate repository and
transiently activates representable ordered subsets for repeated configuration
search, rather than installing one persistent restored state.
\end{sloppypar}

\subsection{Position of \texorpdfstring{\texttt{pg-extstats-advisor}}{pg-extstats-advisor}}

Production/test-server separation, offline advisor execution, recommendation
generation, and DBA-controlled deployment are established systems patterns.
The architecture contribution here is not that offline tuning exists; it is
that the PostgreSQL patch is confined to the private advisor environment while
the validated production target remains stock PostgreSQL.  Capture supplies a
persisted sample and compatibility metadata, the private advisor reconstructs
the fixed realization and payload repository, and deployment returns to
ordinary stock-PostgreSQL DDL under DBA control.

Our earlier CE-Replay line explored the opposite boundary by externalizing a
bounded representation of PostgreSQL CE semantics.  The current system
instead retains PostgreSQL as the semantic authority and virtualizes the
native statistics state it consumes.  CE-Replay is historical context, not a
current runtime contribution or a competing evidence claim.

Accordingly, the inherited prior art includes workload-driven statistics
selection, maintenance-aware recommendation, contextual statistics
interactions, what-if physical design, planner-visible hypothetical objects,
native optimizer evaluation, pending/imported/restored statistics, statistical
views, and offline/test-server tuning.  Within PostgreSQL 16.14's validated
base-relation fragment, our contribution is a backend-local what-if substrate
that binds advisor-derived native MCV/FD payload states (including
\texttt{ABSENT\_NATIVE}) to candidate objects, exposes an ordered active subset
to PostgreSQL's statistics path, and leaves estimator semantics native.  The
claim is deliberately bounded to the fixed target, arity-two candidates,
representable catalog subsets, and the documented predicate fragment; it is
not a claim of firstness, uniqueness, generic PostgreSQL support, or a new
statistics-selection algorithm.
