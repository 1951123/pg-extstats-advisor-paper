\section{Problem and System Contract}
\label{sec:problem}

This section fixes the notation used by the native substrate and evaluator.
The formulation fixes the system contract; workload-driven statistics
selection itself is established prior art.  The variables below describe one
bounded advisor run, not a new optimization formulation or a claim of global
search optimality.

\subsection{Workload and truth}

The advisor receives a fixed supplied workload
\[
 Q=\{q_1,\ldots,q_m\}.
\]
Each query $q\in Q$ contains SQL and its supported predicate context, names
one target base relation, and is bound to an externally supplied exact
cardinality truth $N_q>0$.  The current product objective has no additional
workload-weight field: query contributions are aggregated as a direct sum in
query-identity order.  Truth is therefore an input to the contract, not a
quantity collected or inferred by the private advisor.

The scope is one target base relation and base-relation selection cardinality
estimation.  A query may contain the supported predicates over that relation,
but joins and multi-table execution are outside this contract.  The workload
identity binds query IDs, SQL, effective membership, target relation, and
truth identity; display order is not a semantic input.

\subsection{Candidate catalog and realization}

The workload- and dataset-dependent candidate catalog is
\[
 S=\{s_1,\ldots,s_n\}.
\]
Each candidate $s\in S$ identifies one relation and exactly two attributes,
selects either PostgreSQL MCV or functional-dependency (``dependencies'')
statistics, and carries a deterministic statistics-object definition.  The
definition includes the catalog identity used by the advisor and a unique
precedence rank.  It also has a maintenance cost under the fixed model below.
The candidate universe is not a product constant: benchmark-specific counts
such as 72 or 4,506 describe particular catalogs rather than supported limits.

Let $R$ denote one \emph{advisor-derived statistics realization reconstructed
from a persisted capture sample and compatible metadata}.  $R$ is not the
production capture bundle itself and is not a promise about a future native
\texttt{ANALYZE}.  It includes the ordinary statistics state and the native
candidate payload states derived from that persisted sample.  Once a run
starts, $R$ is fixed; the search does not resample or regenerate it for an
individual design.

For every candidate $s$, define
\[
 P_s(R)\in\{\operatorname{PRESENT}(p_s),\operatorname{ABSENT\_NATIVE}\}.
\]
Here $p_s$ is the non-empty native serialized payload produced by PostgreSQL
for $s$ under $R$.  \texttt{ABSENT\_NATIVE} records that the candidate was
registered and PostgreSQL legitimately produced no requested payload field;
it is not an empty fabricated byte string.  \texttt{UNREGISTERED} is excluded
from this mathematical domain: it is an acquisition, identity, or validation
failure and therefore a system-invalid state rather than a statistical
outcome.

The distinction is operationally important.  A selected
\texttt{ABSENT\_NATIVE} candidate remains a member of the design and has no
fabricated payload; its maintenance cost is still charged to the candidate
definition.  The adapter fails closed on \texttt{UNREGISTERED} rather than
silently dropping the candidate.

\subsection{Design and objective}

A design is a membership set $Y\subseteq S$.  The system does not optimize an
arbitrary permutation.  Instead, the catalog's unique precedence ranks derive
the effective activation order
\[
 \pi(Y)=\operatorname{sort}_{\mathrm{precedence}}(Y),
\]
which is the ordered OID subset passed to the backend-local overlay.  Thus $Y$
specifies membership while $\pi(Y)$ specifies the deterministic PostgreSQL
ordering used for that membership.

For fixed $R$, $Y$, and $\pi(Y)$, let
\[
 \widehat{N}_q(Y,R)
\]
denote the estimate returned by PostgreSQL's native cardinality estimator for
the target relation of query $q$.  This is an interface to native PostgreSQL
through \texttt{EXPLAIN (FORMAT JSON)}, not a learned model or an external
MCV/FD estimator.  The evaluator extracts the unique target-relation
\texttt{Plan Rows} value and combines it with the externally supplied truth.

For positive truth $N_q$, the supported q-error contribution is
\[
 qerr(q,Y,R)=
 \max\left(
 \frac{\max(\widehat{N}_q(Y,R),1)}{N_q},
 \frac{N_q}{\max(\widehat{N}_q(Y,R),1)}
 \right).
\]
The workload loss is the direct aggregate used by the implementation:
\[
 L(Y;R)=\sum_{q\in Q} qerr(q,Y,R).
\]
There is no implicit averaging, normalization, or unimplemented query weight
in this contract.

\subsection{Maintenance constraint and fixed target}

Let $c(s)$ be the cost assigned by the calibrated maintenance model to
candidate definition $s$, and let
\[
 C(Y)=\sum_{s\in Y}c(s).
\]
The feasible designs satisfy $C(Y)\le B$.  Cost is attached to the
definition's mechanism and attributes under the fixed model; it does not
change when $P_s(R)$ is \texttt{PRESENT} versus \texttt{ABSENT\_NATIVE}.
The coefficients are target- and environment-specific resource parameters,
not a universal PostgreSQL maintenance law or a causal per-candidate
\texttt{ANALYZE} model.

The global statistics target is an externally supplied configuration identity:
\[
 T=100\quad\text{by default}.
\]
The caller may supply another compatible positive target, but $T$ is fixed
before capture, realization, advice, and search; it is not an optimization
variable.  Payload realization depends on $T$, and maintenance-model
compatibility is also target-specific.  The target is consequently bound into
the problem, acquisition provenance, cache identity, and search configuration.
An incompatible target or target-specific maintenance model fails closed;
the workflow never silently reuses a $T=100$ model for another $T$.

\subsection{System contract and correctness boundary}

The bounded optimization problem is therefore:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\text{given } Q,\;N,\;S,\;R,\;T,\;c,\;B,\\
 &\underset{Y\subseteq S}{\operatorname{minimize}}\quad L(Y;R)\\
 &\text{subject to}\quad C(Y)\le B,\quad \pi(Y)\text{ fixed by catalog precedence}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
This formalizes the current system contract.  Resource-constrained subset
selection is established physical-design/statistics-management prior art; the
paper does not claim formulation novelty, a global optimum, or an
approximation guarantee.

The correctness contract is same-realization correctness.  Fix one $R$, one
design $Y$, the same ordinary-statistics state, the same candidate payload
states $P_s(R)$, and the same effective order $\pi(Y)$.  Within the supported
PostgreSQL 16.14 fragment, the hypothetical and physical paths should agree
on the target-relation estimate vector and on $L(Y;R)$.  This does not require
or imply that $P_s(R)$ equals the payload produced by a future fresh native
\texttt{ANALYZE}.

Robustness is a separate contract.  Given independent advisor-derived
realizations $R_1,\ldots,R_k$, the evaluation may compare a fixed design's
loss, the selected-design identities, or cross-evaluated losses.  Correctness
is not robustness: the same-realization equality is a semantic fidelity claim.
Robustness is not design invariance either: different realizations may produce
different payloads, estimates, or selected memberships while remaining
consistent with the correctness contract.

Finally, the production/advisor boundary is explicit.  Production capture
supplies the persisted sample, workload, exact truth, schema, and compatibility
metadata.  The private advisor derives the ordinary statistics realization,
the candidate payload repository, and the hypothetical search state.  Native
payload bytes are not exported by the production capture bundle, and the
capture contract does not claim native-\texttt{ANALYZE} sample equivalence.

The contract is PostgreSQL 16.14 only, one base relation, fixed target,
arity-two MCV/FD candidates, and supported base-relation predicates.  Joins,
higher arity, other mechanisms, automatic target selection, latency
objectives, global optimality, sample equivalence across fresh realizations,
and design invariance are outside the claims.
