\section{Discussion, Limitations, and Conclusion}

\subsection{Discussion and Design Implications}

The central design lesson is to \emph{virtualize statistics state, not
cardinality-estimator semantics}.  In this setting, the useful abstraction
boundary was the optimizer input state rather than a reimplementation of
optimizer semantics.  Candidate MCV/FD payloads are derived under one frozen
realization, retained with their identity and possible
\texttt{ABSENT\_NATIVE} state, and exposed as representable ordered subsets.
PostgreSQL remains responsible for the supported applicability, precedence,
consumption, deserialization, composition, and cardinality-estimation rules.
The advisor changes the state consumed by those rules; it does not duplicate
them in an external estimator.

The evidence supports this boundary within the validated fragment.  The
hypothetical and physical paths agree when they use the same realization, one
repository supports many design evaluations without per-design search-loop
materialization, and the tested DMV/Census workloads show contextual rather
than configuration-independent candidate utility.  The invasive mechanism can
remain in a private advisor while capture and deployment use stock PostgreSQL.
The tested DMV studies also show that a fixed design can retain benefit across
independent realizations while independently selected designs can differ
without large cross-sample objective gaps.  These observations are bounded
evidence for the current workflow, not a general theory of database
virtualization or a claim that designs are invariant.

This separation has an operational implication.  The patched PostgreSQL
backend is an evaluation dependency, while production remains a stock
dependency: it constructs the private realization, evaluates recommendations,
and then gives the DBA ordinary stock-PostgreSQL DDL.  Production/test-server
separation remains an established pattern, and the prototype is not
production-ready; the mechanism is usable without requiring a patched
production DBMS.

PostgreSQL 19's restored extended-statistics support demonstrates that native
extended-statistics state is becoming more directly manipulable
\cite{postgresql19restore}.  Our validated PG16.14 substrate addresses a
different state-lifetime requirement: retaining a candidate repository and
transiently activating alternative subsets during search.  Whether a future
upstream PostgreSQL interface could replace some or all of the current patch
is an engineering question outside this paper.

\subsection{Limitations and Future Work}

The scope is PostgreSQL 16.14, one base relation, base-relation selection
estimation, arity-two MCV/dependency statistics, a fixed external target, and
the documented predicate fragment; joins, higher arities, other kinds, and
target optimization are not evaluated.  The release-qualified search is a
deterministic contextual ADD-only construction, secondary to the substrate and
without a global-optimum or approximation guarantee; ADD/DROP/SWAP remains
development/library functionality.  Its maintenance model is target- and
environment-specific, not a universal PostgreSQL law.

The performance evidence is bounded: a DMV materialization-cost pilot
supplements operation counts but establishes no general end-to-end or
Census-scale speedup, and q-error improvement is not a query-latency result.
Same-realization correctness does not imply equality after a fresh native
\texttt{ANALYZE}.  Robustness evidence is DMV-only: selected designs can
differ, and the paper does not study design invariance, temporal or schema
drift, or cross-version robustness.

Deployment is DBA-controlled/manual; rollback removes recommendation
definitions but not a prior realization.  The synthetic clean-room is a
research prototype, not production readiness.  Capture may contain sensitive
SQL, samples, constants, and truth; security and retention are external.
Workload-driven selection, hypothetical objects, native what-if analysis,
statistics import/restore, and offline tuning are prior art; the contribution
is bounded to the native-state workflow above.

Future work may extend the supported boundary to joins, higher-arity or
additional extended-statistics mechanisms, richer search consumers,
alternative fixed targets, broader realization and drift evaluation, and
future upstream interfaces where feasible.  These are directions, not claims
of the current system, and do not reopen the closed sampling-target scope.

\subsection{Conclusion}

PostgreSQL extended-statistics search needs configuration evaluation without
duplicating the database's estimator semantics.  This paper contributes a
backend-local overlay that exposes supported native MCV/FD configurations to
PostgreSQL's own cardinality estimator while keeping candidate realization,
activation order, and production deployment explicit.  The evaluation
establishes same-realization fidelity, contextual design behavior, bounded
advisor-work evidence, production-separated lifecycle closure, and bounded
DMV realization robustness.  The contribution remains deliberately scoped to
the fixed-target PG16.14 base-relation fragment and its representable
candidate catalog.  For PostgreSQL extended statistics in the validated
fragment, virtualizing estimator state provides a practical what-if boundary
while leaving PostgreSQL itself as the semantic authority.
